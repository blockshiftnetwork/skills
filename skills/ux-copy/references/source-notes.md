# Source notes and further reading

This skill synthesizes the sources below; it does not reproduce them. Use product research and verified implementation behavior over generic guidance when they conflict. Standards are requirements only when the product's target, policy, or jurisdiction makes them applicable.

## Accessibility and inclusive interaction

- [Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/) — labels and instructions, error identification and suggestions, error prevention for consequential submissions, and programmatically available status messages.
- [Understanding SC 3.3.4: Error Prevention (Legal, Financial, Data)](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/error-prevention-legal-financial-data.html) — reversal, checking, or review before important submissions.
- [Google: Write accessible documentation](https://developers.google.com/style/accessibility) — accessible labels, error guidance, descriptive control references, and alternatives to visual-only cues.
- [Apple Human Interface Guidelines: Inclusion](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/inclusion) — direct, respectful, welcoming language.

## Interface writing and content patterns

- [Apple Human Interface Guidelines: Writing](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/writing) — action orientation, clear labels, consistent language patterns, and useful errors.
- [Apple Human Interface Guidelines: Alerts](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/alerts) — specific alert titles and result-oriented button labels.
- [Microsoft: Writing style for Windows apps](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/apps/design/style/writing-style) — voice, UI text, errors, dialogs, buttons, accessibility, and localization.
- [Microsoft Writing Style Guide](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/style-guide/welcome/) — clear, concise technology writing and global communication.
- [GOV.UK Design System: Error message](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/components/error-message/) and [Error summary](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/components/error-summary/) — specific, consistent error recovery and accessible summaries.
- [Atlassian Design System: Content design](https://atlassian.design/get-started/content-design) and [Writing a warning message](https://atlassian.design/content/writing-guidelines/writing-a-warning-message) — product content standards, warnings, reversibility, and scannability.
- [Nielsen Norman Group: Error-Message Guidelines](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/error-message-guidelines/) — visible, constructive, human-readable recovery messages.
- [Nielsen Norman Group: How Users Read on the Web](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/how-users-read-on-the-web/) — scannability, front-loading, and concise factual content.

## Localization

- [Microsoft Globalization: Software internationalization](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/globalization/methodology/software-internationalization) — externalized strings, translator context, and responsive layouts for expansion.
- [Microsoft Globalization: String concatenation](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/globalization/internationalization/concatenation) — grammatical and punctuation failures caused by assembled messages.
- [Google: Write for a global audience](https://developers.google.com/style/translation) — consistent, direct language without culture-specific idioms or humor.
- [Unicode LDML MessageFormat](https://www.unicode.org/reports/tr35/tr35-messageFormat.html) — locale-sensitive selection and plural rules.

## AI, privacy, and trust

- [Microsoft HAX Toolkit: Guidelines for Human-AI Interaction](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/haxtoolkit/ai-guidelines/) — capability and performance expectations, context, error handling, controls, and explanations.
- [Apple Human Interface Guidelines: Generative AI](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/generative-ai/) — capability, privacy, disclosure, and user control for AI features.
- [Apple Human Interface Guidelines: Privacy](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/privacy/) — contextual, specific permission requests and transparent data use.
- [Federal Trade Commission: Bringing Dark Patterns to Light](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2022/09/ftc-report-shows-rise-sophisticated-dark-patterns-designed-trick-trap-consumers) — misleading interfaces, hidden terms, false hierarchy, and difficult cancellation.

## Research and measurement

- [GOV.UK Service Manual: Using moderated usability testing](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/user-research/using-moderated-usability-testing) — realistic tasks and observation of comprehension and completion.
- [GOV.UK Service Manual: Usability benchmarking](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/measuring-success/usability-benchmarking-a-website-or-whole-service) — repeatable task success evidence combined with research.
- [Digital.gov: Test for understanding](https://digital.gov/guides/plain-language/test/usability-testing) — evaluating whether people can find and understand content as a whole.

## Books

- Torrey Podmajersky, *Strategic Writing for UX*, 2nd ed. (O'Reilly, 2025) — goals, voice, content-first design, recurring UI text patterns, measurement, and AI-generated UX content.
- Michael J. Metts and Andy Welfle, *Writing Is Designing* (Rosenfeld Media, 2020) — treating words as product design, including stress cases, accessibility, collaboration, and testing.
- Kinneret Yifrah, *Microcopy: The Complete Guide*, 2nd ed. (Nemala, 2019) — action, guidance, friction reduction, voice, and microcopy patterns.
- Janice Redish, *Letting Go of the Words*, 2nd ed. (Morgan Kaufmann, 2012) — task-oriented, conversational, scannable content for websites and apps.
- Caroline Jarrett and Gerry Gaffney, *Forms That Work* (Morgan Kaufmann, 2009) — the relationship, conversation, and presentation layers of usable forms.
